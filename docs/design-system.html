The law: never show a number you can't defend

A percentage exists only where a real denominator does. Everywhere else the interface shows a count, or a dash. It never shows 0% to mean "we don't know yet", and it never averages percentages across goals that measure different things.

An untouched today is not a miss. A day still being lived is excluded from every denominator until something is logged against it.

Nothing is inferred from a missing value. If a number wasn't typed, it doesn't exist.

This is not a philosophical preference. The engine this replaced counted every unrecorded duration as 30 minutes, so on real data it reported 250 minutes of walking where 100 had been recorded — it invented 150. "Walk 30 minutes a day" and "visit 10 places" share no denominator, so any single figure combining them is fiction. Design decisions downstream of this rule: the em dash, the count-instead-of-percentage fallback, the hollow final point on a trend line, and the absent column rather than a zero-height one.

Colour

A warm, low-chroma ground with a single terracotta accent. Neutrals are hue-biased toward the accent rather than pure grey, so the whole surface reads as one material. Semantic colours (success, destructive, warm) are reserved for state and never used as decoration.

Data visualisation

Five categorical hues, assigned in fixed order and never cycled. Two things about them are deliberate and easy to get wrong.

Lightness is staggered, not uniform. Adjacent slots differ in L, because lightness is what survives colour-vision deficiency — hue alone does not. A palette of five equally-bright hues collapses into near-identical greys under deuteranopia.

Dark is re-stepped, not flipped. The warm hues take the low slots in dark mode and the cool ones the high slots, because cyan cannot hold the chroma floor when it is dark.

Rules for charts

  • One series, one hue. Colour is an encoding. A ranked list of one kind of thing gets a single colour; cycling hues down it implies groups that don't exist, and repeats once the rows outnumber the hues.
  • Never a second y-axis. Two measures of different scale become two charts.
  • Gaps stay gaps. A missing value breaks the line; joining across it draws a trend nobody measured.
  • No zero-height marks. An absent column and a measured zero must not look the same.
  • Every chart has a table view, because colour alone is not an encoding.
  • Direction never rests on colour. A delta carries an arrow and a sign as well as a hue.
  • Text wears text tokens, never the series colour.

Typography

Fraunces for display, Plus Jakarta Sans for everything read in quantity. Fraunces carries an optical-size axis, and it is now set per level — without it a 40px heading is drawn with the letterforms of 14px body text, which is the entire reason to choose a variable serif.

Motion and focus

Three animations exist: pop on a completed tap, rise on page entry, glow on a milestone. All of them are now behind prefers-reduced-motion — previously they ran regardless, in an app that pops something on every single tap.

Focus had no styling outside the shadcn primitives, so it disappeared anywhere an outline was reset. There is now a global :focus-visible ring on the accent. The app is operated daily, often one-handed; the focus state is not decoration.

Removed

  • Stats

Two screens of numbers is a defect, not a feature. Stats also carried its own copy of the monthly-savings chart that Money already showed. /stats redirects rather than 404s — it was in the nav for months.

Memories moved into the primary five because keeping one is a daily act in a product about a hundred days of them. Money moved out: it was over-weighted as one of five now that spending is one goal category among many, rather than one of exactly four habits.

Voice

The app talks to one person about their own life, so it uses second person and stays plain. Wording that depends on whether a journey is solo or shared resolves through a single module — never branched inline — because the alternative was a fifth duplicated list of labels.

  • Never imply a second person who isn't there. A solo journey is a complete thing, not a broken shared one. Earlier copy told solo users to "cheer on your partner".
  • Say what happened, not what was attempted. If a template added three goals and skipped two you already had, the toast says so.
  • An empty state offers the next action, not an apology.
  • Encouragement is never a comparison. The other person appears in copy only when it's encouraging, never as a ranking.

Known gaps

  • No spacing scale. Screens still use Tailwind's defaults ad hoc; the type scale landed first because it was doing more visible damage.
  • The mock backend ships to production. The data facade imports both backends unconditionally, so demo fixtures sit in the bundle — about 9 KB gzipped. Dead, not reachable, but shipped.
  • No component gallery in-app. This page documents the system; nothing renders it live against the real components, so drift is possible.
  • Warm has no dark-mode contrast partner defined for text-on-warm at small sizes — it passes today at 9.79:1, but that is luck rather than a stated rule.
